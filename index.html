The app is free. Every course, every question, every feature: there is no purchase and no subscription.

How it works

You read a course, and the app asks you about it later. Questions are multiple choice wherever that can be done fairly: a glossary term shows its definition and you pick the word, a diagram shows itself and you pick what it is, and a gap fill takes a sentence you have already met and removes one word from it.

Every answer, right or wrong, then shows you what all four options meant, so one question teaches four things. Get one wrong and the app names the slide it came from and offers to open it.

Spaced repetition decides what comes back and when. Answer quickly and it waits longer; get it wrong and it returns sooner.

The Practice tab adds exam-style questions for every course: a stem, four options, and an explanation of why each wrong answer is wrong.

See the techniques work

Animations of the methods themselves, stage by stage: Sanger, Illumina, PacBio and Nanopore sequencing; PCR, RT-PCR and qPCR; bright field, phase contrast, DIC, fluorescence and confocal microscopes; reporter genes; CRISPR-Cas9; and serial dilution. Watch them here, or in the app.

Courses for enrolled students

The Courses tab holds course material, revision decks, data interpretation workshops and exam-style practice for students on particular courses. It opens with an access code their course provides. Everything else in the app is open to everyone.

Built from research papers

Every course is written from open access research papers. Each figure is drawn from numbers in the source, the papers are named on the first slide of each course and open with one tap, and where a slide states standard textbook material it says so.

The app also searches Europe PMC, so you can find and read full papers without a paywall or an institutional login.

Privacy, briefly

No accounts, no analytics, no tracking, no advertising. Your progress stays on your device. Everything except the paper search works with no network at all. The full policy says the same thing at greater length.
